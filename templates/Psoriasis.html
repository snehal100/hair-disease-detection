Psoriasis

Symptoms

  • Red patches of skin covered with thick, silvery scales
  • Dry, cracked skin that may bleed
  • Itching, burning, or soreness
  • Swollen and stiff joints
  • Scalp scaling that may extend beyond the hairline
  • Thickened, pitted, discolored, or separating nails
  • Dry plaques that crack and bleed when scratched
  • Small scattered spots after an infection, especially in children
  • Symptoms that flare and improve over time
  • Joint pain, stiffness, or swelling that may indicate psoriatic arthritis

Causes

  • Psoriasis happens when the immune system becomes overactive.
  • Family history can increase the risk.
  • Stress may make it worse.
  • Some infections or medicines may trigger it.
  • Smoking may make symptoms worse.

Treatments

  • Topical treatments (steroids, Vitamin D analogues)
  • Phototherapy (light therapy)
  • Systemic medications (oral or injected)
  • Biologic drugs for moderate to severe cases
  • Moisturizers, emollients, and gentle fragrance-free cleansers
  • Medicated shampoos for scalp psoriasis, used as directed
  • Prescription medicines such as retinoids, methotrexate, or cyclosporine in selected cases
  • Stress management, avoiding personal triggers, and stopping tobacco use
  • Regular follow-up when joint symptoms or extensive skin involvement occurs

Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory condition and is not contagious. A dermatologist can tailor treatment to the severity, body area involved, and presence of joint symptoms.
