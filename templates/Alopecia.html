Alopecia Areata

Symptoms

  • Sudden hair loss in small patches on the scalp, face, or body
  • Thinning hair
  • Tingling or itching in the affected areas
  • Smooth, round patches where the skin may look normal
  • Short broken hairs or tapering “exclamation mark” hairs near a patch
  • Loss of eyelashes, eyebrows, beard, or other body hair
  • Small dents, ridges, or roughness in the fingernails or toenails
  • Hair loss that may come and go or spread to larger areas

Causes

  • The body's immune system attacks the hair roots.
  • Family history can increase the risk.
  • Stress may sometimes cause hair loss.
  • The exact cause is not always known.

Treatments

  • Steroid creams or injections may help.
  • Minoxidil may help hair grow.
  • Some medicines may be given by a doctor.
  • Severe cases may need special medicines.
  • A wig or hairpiece can be used if needed.
  • Protect the scalp from the sun and cold.
  • See a dermatologist for the right treatment.

Treatment depends on the person’s age, the amount of hair loss, and how quickly it is progressing. A dermatologist can confirm the diagnosis and discuss risks and benefits.
