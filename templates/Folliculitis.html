Folliculitis

Symptoms

  • Small red or white-headed pimples around hair follicles
  • Itchy or burning skin
  • Tenderness in affected areas
  • Pus-filled blisters that break open and crust over
  • Clusters of bumps on the scalp, face, neck, chest, back, or thighs
  • Skin that feels painful or warm around the affected follicles
  • Temporary hair loss or small areas of scarring in severe scalp cases
  • Fever, spreading redness, or increasing pain when infection is more serious

Causes

  • Bacteria or fungi can cause the problem.
  • Shaving can irritate the skin.
  • Waxing can cause ingrown hairs.
  • Tight clothes can rub the skin.
  • Sweating can make the problem worse.
  • Blocked hair follicles can cause infection.

Treatments

  • Antibiotic or antifungal creams
  • Topical antiseptics such as hydrogen peroxide
  • Good hygiene and warm compresses
  • Oral antibiotics for severe cases
  • Antifungal shampoo or oral antifungal medicine when yeast is involved
  • Prescription anti-inflammatory lotions to reduce irritation
  • Pausing shaving, waxing, or friction until the skin heals
  • Washing towels, pillowcases, clothing, and sports equipment regularly
  • Laser hair removal for some recurring cases after medical evaluation

The correct treatment depends on whether bacteria, fungi, irritation, or an ingrown hair is responsible. Seek medical care if redness spreads, fever develops, or symptoms do not improve.
